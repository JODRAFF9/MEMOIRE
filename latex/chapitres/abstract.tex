\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

In Senegal, children remain deprived across a range of dimensions that
undermine their well-being: more than half of them experienced
multidimensional poverty in 2018, while close to a quarter of households
receive migrant remittances. This study evaluates the impact of sustained
exposure to remittances on children's multidimensional poverty. It draws
on the two waves of the Harmonized Household Living Standards Survey
(EHCVM~I, 2018-2019 and EHCVM~II, 2021-2022). Child poverty is measured
through UNICEF's Multiple Overlapping Deprivation Analysis (MODA), with
seven dimensions and thirteen indicators tailored to three age groups
(0-4, 5-14 and 15-17 years), and the outcome is the number of deprived
dimensions. Treatment is defined as continuous exposure: households
receiving remittances in both waves are compared with never-recipient
households. The effect is estimated by propensity score matching on
second-wave deprivations, with the baseline deprivation count included in
the score, separately within each age group; the robustness of the result is
probed through the treatment definition and the deprivation cutoff.

Descriptive results show that the incidence of multidimensional poverty
declined from 60.4 to 51.9 percent between the two waves, and that
children in recipient households start from a much lower deprivation
level, 3.1 deprivations on average against 4.2, an advantage that
predates treatment. After matching, remittances reduce the number of
deprivations by 0.19 among children aged 5 to 14, significant at the ten
percent level; the effect has the same sign but is imprecise among
children aged 0 to 4, and the small number of treated adolescents
precludes any conclusion. These findings call for stronger complementarity between
private transfers and the public supply of basic services, particular
attention to preschool-age children, among whom the protective effect
does not materialize, and keeping recipient households within the scope
of social protection.

\bigskip

\noindent \textbf{Keywords}: child multidimensional poverty, MODA,
remittances, Senegal, EHCVM, propensity score matching.
