\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

In Senegal, more than half of all children were multidimensionally poor in
2018, while nearly one household in four (24\%) receives migrant
remittances. This study estimates the causal impact of remittances on child
multidimensional poverty, using the two rounds of the Harmonized Household
Living Standards Survey (EHCVM~I, 2018-2019 and EHCVM~II, 2021-2022).
Poverty is measured through UNICEF's MODA approach (seven dimensions,
14 indicators, three age groups), and identification combines propensity
score matching with difference-in-differences (PSM-DiD). The unit of
analysis is the child: the score and the matching are estimated on the
17,786 children followed across both rounds. Treated households are those
receiving, in 2018, a transfer from a sender living outside Senegal who had
previously been a member of the household - 13.9\% of households and
14.8\% of the children followed.

Incidence falls from 63.5\% to 57.8\% between the two rounds, and children
in recipient households are less often poor than others at both dates. That
level advantage, however, predates treatment: once matching is applied, the
average treatment effect on the treated stands at 0.058, significant at the
10\% level and unfavourable in sign. Deprivation among children of
recipient households therefore declines more slowly than among comparable
children, a result stable across the three matching methods. The hypothesis
that remittances reduce child poverty is rejected. The effect is
established only for nutrition (8.1~pp) and among children aged 0 to 4
(7.9~pp), while no equality test points to a differentiated effect by area,
sex or age group; it concentrates on the smallest amounts received. This
pattern points to the cost of the departed adult's absence, though that
channel cannot carry the gap on its own. These results call for lower
remittance transaction costs, stronger complementarity between private
transfers and public provision of basic services, and targeted support for
early childhood in households whose adult is absent through migration.

\bigskip

\noindent \textbf{Keywords}: child multidimensional poverty, MODA, remittances,
Senegal, EHCVM, propensity score matching (PSM), difference-in-differences (DiD).
